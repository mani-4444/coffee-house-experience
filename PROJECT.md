# One by Two — Coffee House, Hyderabad

A single-page marketing site for a fictional specialty coffee house in Hyderabad. It is built to the standard of a real client engagement and could be handed to an owner.

> All business details (people, estates, phone, email, prices) are fictional, and the location is given only as Hyderabad. They live in `src/data/` and must be replaced before launch.

## Problem

Hyderabad's new specialty cafés all look the same: Japandi interiors, oat-milk menus, Instagram walls. Their websites look the same too. A café that is *different in the room* needs a site that shows the difference before anyone walks in. A list of menu items and a hero photo can't do that.

## Business goal

In priority order, the site should make a visitor:

1. **Come in.** Find the café, see whether it is open now, and get directions in one tap.
2. **Know what to order.** Understand the signature (*kaapi, by two*) before they arrive.
3. **Bring someone.** The ritual is built for two, so the site sells the second visitor.
4. **Book the long table.** It is the only reservable seat, for groups, book clubs and Sunday book-market crowds.

Non-goals: online ordering, delivery, e-commerce, loyalty.

## Target audience

| Group | Who | What they need from the site |
|---|---|---|
| Old-city regulars | 35–65, grew up on Irani chai in the old city's cafés | Reassurance that it is still a café, not a lounge. Prices. Hours. |
| Young specialty drinkers | 20–32, from Gachibowli, Banjara Hills, students | A reason to cross the city: the signature drink, the origin story, the room. |
| Sunday book-market crowd | All ages, on Sunday mornings | Is it open at 7? Can I sit with my books? |
| Groups / book clubs | 6–10 people | How to book the long table. |
| Visitors to Hyderabad | Tourists, business travellers | Where it is, what's local about it, what to order. |

## Brand premise

**Name:** One by Two (mark: **½**). Descriptor: *Coffee House · Hyderabad.*

**The name:** In Hyderabad's Irani cafés you order chai *"one by two"*: one cup poured into two glasses and shared. Our café applies that custom to South Indian filter coffee.

**Location:** Hyderabad. The site names only the city (client request); a restored 1950s shopfront in the old city, near a Sunday book market.

**Story:** It is two families' trades, joined. Shirin Darashah's family ran an Irani café in Hyderabad's old city for three generations. Kiran Malnad's family has grown arabica on the Mallige Hills estate for four. The house decoction is two coffees blended: the Malnad estate's arabica and a seasonal lot from Neeli Valley hill-farmer cooperatives. It is served the Irani way: marble tables, bentwood chairs, house rules on the wall, no hurry. Opened March 2025.

**Personality:** Warm, dry-witted, unhurried, exact. It has the confidence of an old institution even though it is new. It is never cute, and it never explains itself twice.

**Positioning:** A specialty coffee bar with an Irani café's manners. Serious about coffee, unserious about itself.

**Price perception:** Accessible premium. Kaapi by two costs ₹120 for two cups, so sharing makes specialty coffee cheaper than a chain latte. Estate pour-overs cost ₹240.

**Atmosphere:** Morning light on white marble. Steel tumblers. A brown enamel sign. Newspapers. The sound of decoction being poured from a height. Conversation, not laptops, after 5 pm.

**Signature product:** **Kaapi, by two:** one filter-coffee decoction split into two steel tumblers and davaras at the table, with chicory (20%) or without.

**Differentiator:** The café is built around sharing. The menu, the room (one long table) and the rules all encourage you to come with someone. No other café in the city says so out loud.

## Success looks like

A café owner reads the site and recognises their own café: specific, local, with a point of view. A customer leaves knowing where to go, when, what to order and who to bring.
