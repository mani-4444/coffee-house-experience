The house order

Kaapi, by two.

Irani cafés have always split a chai between two glasses. We do the same with South Indian filter coffee: one decoction, poured at your table into two tumblers.

Two cups for ₹120. Bring someone, or make a friend; the tables are small. With chicory, twenty percent, the old way, or without. Ask for it strong and we will know you grew up with it.

House rules

Est. 2025Some inherited, some new. All enforced, gently.

  1. 01

    One coffee may be shared between two. We will bring the second tumbler.

  2. 02

    Decoction drips for eleven minutes. Some things cannot be hurried.

  3. 03

    Laptops are welcome until 5 pm. After that, the tables belong to conversation.

  4. 04

    Dunking is permitted. Osmania biscuits were made for it.

  5. 05

    On Sundays we open at 7 for the book market. Show us what you found.

  6. 06

    No reserved seats, except the long table. Ask us nicely.

  7. 07

    Speak softly before 9 am. The regulars are reading.

  8. 08

    No credit. No combing hair at the table. Some rules we inherited.

Behind the counter

Two trades, one counter.

One family made chai for three generations. The other grew coffee for four. In 2025 they took a shopfront in Hyderabad and split it down the middle, like everything else here.

The chai side

Shirin Darashah

Third generation · Hyderabad

Her great-grandfather came to Hyderabad from Persia in 1936 and opened a tea room in the old city. Shirin brought what the family kept: the marble tops, the bentwood chairs, the rules on the wall, and the Osmania biscuit recipe, unchanged.

The coffee side

Kiran Malnad

Fourth generation · Mallige Hills

His family has grown arabica on the Mallige Hills estate for four generations, on slopes where the morning mist sits in the shade trees until noon. Kiran roasts for the café every Thursday and drives the beans in himself.
